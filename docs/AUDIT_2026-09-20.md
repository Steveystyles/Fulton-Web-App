# Server, website and content audit — 20 September 2026

## Verdict

The deployed website is available, storage is healthy, and the application passes its automated regression suite. It is **not fully up to date**, and neither content pipeline earns an unconditional quality or reliability sign-off. The map fails parts of its documented editorial rubric and its seven-round queue target. The quiz has functioning factual safeguards but excessive retries, late publication, uneven difficulty and repetitive content.

This report contains past and upcoming puzzle spoilers. Production inspection was read-only. No accounts, scores, published questions, clues, provider settings, dependencies or server packages were changed.

## Priority findings

### 1. Map supply is below its operating target

There are **zero ready automatic-map entries**, against `TARGET_READY = 7` in `tools/automatic_map_pool.py`. All nine entries in the queue tables have been consumed: seven discovered and two bootstrap entries. Published map puzzles exist for every date September 12–22, so the empty queue is not an immediate player outage.

From September 8 through the observed early September 20 runs, discovery added seven rounds from 51 attempts, approximately 14%. The queue is being consumed faster than it fills. Reviewed fallback content supplies some dates, including September 16, 20 and 22. The runtime safely rejects failed discovery rather than publishing it, but the desired self-sustaining supply has not been demonstrated.

Recent rejection reports identify missing/ambiguous pages, non-unique coordinates, article/search-area mismatches, used place types, and Wikipedia/Wikidata coordinates differing by more than 250 metres. Keukenhof repeatedly returns despite prior failures. Failed names are supplied to the model as prompt exclusions; `discover_map_places.discover()` does not directly reject an excluded proposal before source work. This wastes the tightly bounded daily attempt allowance.

Recommended: enforce exclusions using canonical identity, improve source/title resolution and candidate preflight, then measure replenishment on an isolated evaluation database. Preserve the request budgets and factual gates. Alert on actual ready stock and published coverage, rather than health alone.

### 2. The automatic map path does not consistently meet the fun rubric

The written target in `MAP_CLUE_GRADING_MATRIX.md` is a fun but beatable adult general-knowledge challenge: memorable place-owned hard hook, useful geographic narrowing, then practical phone-map placement.

Observed editorial assessment:

| Round | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| CN Tower, September 14 | Hard clue discusses broadcast obstruction without a clear, memorable event or even an explicit target reference. Weak recognition hook. |
| Edinburgh Castle, September 15 | Hard clue is geological age/location trivia, not the rubric's event/story hook. |
| Palace of Westminster, September 17 | Strong tally-stick fire story; a good example of the desired hook. |
| Neues Museum, September 18 | Construction technique is niche; easy clue points to Museum Island, which contains several museums. It does not uniquely guide the final pin. |
| Neuschwanstein Castle, September 19 | Recognisable Wagner association and useful location, but the hard clue is a fragment about commissioning rather than a developed incident. Qualified editorial pass. |
| Panathenaic Stadium, September 21 | Hard clue is a material/superlative fact. The round is stored as `engineering`, bypassing the automatic discovery policy excluding stadiums. |

Seven discovered entries have stored independent-request verification verdicts and payload hashes. They are machine-checked, not human-approved. The verifier uses the same model family and returns broad booleans such as `interesting_hook`; these do not reliably enforce the full editorial matrix. Published pool metadata says `clue_critic_mode=local`, so the separate verifier record is needed to understand what actually ran.

The medium clue is deliberately templated in `discover_map_places.py` as a city-and-country instruction. That narrows geography but conflicts with the older rubric's preference for a clue rather than a direct instruction. The product policy and prompt need to agree explicitly.

The reviewed fallback route also needs re-auditing. The September 22 Wembley clue describes 1966 as happening on “this pitch”, without distinguishing the old and rebuilt stadium; its A406 direction needs a map check before that round is served. Existing “reviewed” status should not substitute for checking current wording and geography.

Recommended: validate actual place classification, apply one explicit editorial standard to discovery and fallback paths, test complete clue ladders with plausible alternative targets, and manually review the already prepared questionable rounds through a version-safe content workflow. Do not directly overwrite played content.

### 3. Quiz generation is inefficient and sometimes late

All nine September 12–20 quiz dates are published with six questions: one easy, medium and hard for each subject. The September 21 set was still generating with one question at inspection, before its play day.

There were **2,576 rejected attempt records for 54 accepted questions** across September 12–20, approximately 2.1% accepted. Attempt records are not equivalent to billable API calls or token cost. Rejections include repeated answers, excessive length, volatile facts, unsupported evidence, incorrect generated answers and provider quota errors.

Three sets were late relative to their Europe/London play day:

| Quiz date | Published local time |
| --- | --- |
| September 15 | 08:27 BST |
| September 16 | 01:37 BST |
| September 18 | 08:51 BST |

Production has `QUIZ_EVIDENCE_REQUIRED=1` and `QUIZ_CANDIDATE_PIPELINE_ENABLED=0`. These sets identify their pipeline as `legacy`. The newer discovery/composition candidate pipeline is present in the repository but is not the live path. It should not be enabled merely because it exists; compare it against the live path using isolated, reviewed evaluations first.

Recommended: address repeated answer/topic proposals before generation and evidence retrieval, measure retries and elapsed time per slot, and introduce publication-deadline alerting. Keep evidence and critic rejection intact.

### 4. Quiz critic scores overstate editorial quality

Reviewed 24 published questions from September 17–20. All have stored evidence and verification marked passed; their accepted critic scores meet the configured thresholds (validity 8, correctness 9, difficulty match 8, interestingness 7, non-repetition 9). This proves the gates ran, not that their judgements were correct.

Concrete issues:

- September 19 football has three stadium questions. Its easy Maracanã question also primes the medium question about the “Marakana” nickname. Variety and independent difficulty suffer even though the answers differ.
- September 18's easy football question names “Sanfrecce” in the stem and offers “Sanfrecce Hiroshima” as the answer. This is largely name matching.
- The easy questions about qanats and Athens' Long Walls are more specialist than the medium Saint Bernard question. Difficulty calibration is inconsistent across days.
- September 17's hard answer is literally stored as `Johannes M&uuml;ller`. The player components render strings as text, so the HTML entity is not decoded into Müller.
- The September 18 Bosman question unnecessarily claims a stadium was the site where the ruling originated. The club-transfer dispute is the useful, supported premise; a physical stadium location is not established by the question's reasoning.
- The Highbury question compresses different architects and stands into an imprecise attribution. Arsenal credits Ferrier for the West Stand; Historic England identifies the East Stand as William Binnie's work. See [Arsenal's history](https://www.arsenal.com/feature/highbury-aOvWp5K7lTf9) and [the statutory East Stand listing](https://historicengland.org.uk/listing/the-list/list-entry/1119692). Review the wording rather than treating the critic's 10/10 correctness as proof.
- The generated `interesting_reason` contains much of the memorable payoff, but the player results display `explanation` only (`src/components/DailyQuiz.tsx`). For example, the Saint Bernard barrel/painting story is not delivered through that results component.

Recommended: evaluate a whole subject round for variety and difficulty order; reject answer giveaways and unsupported incidental qualifiers; normalize plain text safely before storing new content; surface a verified interesting payoff after adjudication. Preserve opaque choice IDs and server grading.

## Limits and acceptance decision

Only six historical map guesses exist (five positive scores), which is too small a sample to establish enjoyment, fairness, retention or calibrated solve difficulty. No recent human play-test or fair/unclear feedback sample was collected. The browser session was unauthenticated, so this audit verified the public/login surface and relied on automated contracts plus code/database inspection for authenticated play. It did not complete fresh mobile/keyboard end-to-end gameplay, map-tile-provider testing or a new live submission.
